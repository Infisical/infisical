Access control in PAM happens at two levels:

1. **Product membership** — being added to PAM itself (the "entry ticket")
2. **Folder and account memberships** — being granted a role on specific folders or accounts (this is what lets you connect)

Users, groups, and [machine identities](/documentation/platform/identities/machine-identities) all go through both levels and hold the same roles, so everything below applies to all three unless it says otherwise. An [AI agent](/documentation/platform/pam/ai-agents/overview) runs as whoever started it, so it uses either a person's access or a machine identity's.

## Product membership

Before anyone can do anything in PAM, they need to be added as a product member. This is the "entry ticket" to PAM.

There are two product roles:

| Role | What they can do |
|------|------------------|
| **Product Admin** | Create and manage templates, folders, and product-level settings |
| **Product Member** | Access PAM — needs folder/account memberships to connect |

Product Admins set up the governance layer — they don't automatically get access to connect to accounts. To connect, you need a membership on a folder or account.

<Note>
Organization Admins can enter PAM as a Product Admin without being explicitly added — they just need to opt in.
</Note>

### Adding product members

1. Go to **Privileged Access Management → Access Control**
2. Select the **Users**, **Groups**, or **Identities** tab
3. Click **Add**
4. Select from users, groups, or machine identities in your organization
5. Choose **Product Admin** or **Product Member**
6. Click **Add**

The **Identities** tab can also create a new machine identity for PAM instead of adding an existing one. See [AI Agents](/documentation/platform/pam/ai-agents/overview) for the agent workflow that uses it.

## Folder and account memberships

Once someone is a product member, they still need to be granted access to specific folders or accounts. You do this by assigning them a **role** on a folder or account.

### Roles

| Role | What they can do |
|------|------------------|
| **Admin** | Full control — accounts, folders, sessions, memberships |
| **Operator** | Launch sessions and [view account credentials](/documentation/platform/pam/accounts/viewing-credentials) |
| **Connector** | Launch sessions and connect to accounts |
| **Auditor** | View audit logs and session recordings |

<Note>
  **Operator** is Connector plus the right to read an account's stored password or key. Grant it only where someone genuinely needs the credential in hand, since a revealed credential leaves no session recording. An Operator cannot approve requests or change configuration, so an account gated with **Require Approval for Credentials** still needs someone else to sign off.
</Note>

### Folder vs account memberships

You can assign roles at two levels:

**Folder memberships** apply to all accounts in the folder. This is the primary way to grant access — if someone needs access to a set of accounts, grant them a role on the folder.

**Account memberships** apply to a single account. Use this for exceptions — a contractor who needs just one database, or temporary elevated access to a specific account.

### How to assign memberships

For folders:
1. Open the folder and go to **Permissions**
2. Click **Assign Access**
3. Select a user, group, or machine identity, then set the role and an optional expiration
4. Click **Add**

For accounts:
1. Open the account and go to **Permissions**
2. Same process as above

The picker only offers members of PAM, so a machine identity has to be added on the **Access Control** page before it can be granted a role here.

### What only people can do

Two things stay with users and groups:

- **Web Access.** Connecting in the browser is for users. Machine identities connect through the [CLI](/cli/commands/pam-agentic).
- **Approving [access requests](/documentation/platform/pam/access-requests/overview).** A machine identity can file a request, but only users and groups can approve one.

An account whose [template](/documentation/platform/pam/templates/overview) requires MFA also stays with people: a machine identity can never complete the challenge, and [agent runs](/documentation/platform/pam/ai-agents/overview) do not support it yet.

## Putting it together

Here's a typical setup:

1. **Product Admin** adds the Platform team lead as a Product Member
2. **Product Admin** creates a `platform-team` folder
3. **Product Admin** grants the team lead **Admin** on that folder
4. **Team lead** (now a Folder Admin) adds accounts to the folder
5. **Team lead** grants the rest of the team **Connector** on the folder
6. **Team members** can now connect to any account in the folder

The Product Admin sets up the governance structure. The Folder Admin manages day-to-day access within their scope. Team members connect to what they've been granted access to.
