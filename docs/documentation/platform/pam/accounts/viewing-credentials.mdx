Most privileged work in Infisical never needs the password. A member [launches a session](/documentation/platform/pam/sessions/overview), the Gateway injects the credential, and the session is recorded. The credential stays inside the platform.

Some work still needs the credential itself. A DBA has to paste a password into a desktop client that Infisical does not broker, or an engineer has to configure an appliance by hand. Viewing credentials covers that case: a member reads the stored password or key directly, once approved, and the reveal lands in the audit log.

<Warning>
  A revealed credential is outside Infisical. There is no session recording, no command blocking, and no way to end the access early. Prefer a session wherever one will do, and gate credential access with approval where you cannot.
</Warning>

## Who can view a credential

Two things have to hold.

- **Permission.** The member needs the **Operator** or **Admin** [role](/documentation/platform/pam/concepts/access-control) on the folder or account. Connector and Auditor cannot view credentials.
- **Approval**, when the account's [template](/documentation/platform/pam/templates/overview) turns on **Require Approval for Credentials**. The member requests credential access and an approver signs off before the reveal works.

This mirrors how session gating works: permission is the record of who can ever read the credential, and approval decides whether they are cleared to read it right now.

## Viewing a credential

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the account's menu">
    From the **Accounts** page, open the menu on the account row and choose **View Credentials**. On a gated account the entry reads **Request Credentials** instead until you hold an approval.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give a reason">
    Enter why you need the credential. The reason is required when the template turns on **Require Reason**, and is recorded either way.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete MFA, if the template requires it">
    An account whose template turns on **Require MFA** challenges you before the reveal, the same as it does before a session.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read or copy the credential">
    Sensitive fields are masked until you reveal them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Gating credential access with approval

Approval for credentials is configured the same way as approval for sessions, and shares the same approvers.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on the policy (Product Admin)">
    Go to **Privileged Access Management → Account Templates**, open the template for the account type, and turn on **Require Approval for Credentials**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Onboard the account with that template (Folder Admin)">
    Accounts using the template inherit the requirement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Seat approvers in the folder (Folder Admin)">
    Open the folder and go to its **Approvals** tab. The approvers seated there review both session and credential requests for the folder's accounts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A credential request carries a reason and a duration, is reviewed from **Approval Requests** like any other, and shows a **Credentials** badge so an approver can tell it apart from a session request. Once approved, the member can reveal the credential until the duration runs out.

<Note>
  Session and credential grants are independent. An approved session request never reveals the credential, and an approved credential request never launches a session. A member who needs both files both.
</Note>

## Rotate after a checkout

A credential that has left the platform should not stay valid indefinitely. For account types that support it, set up [credential rotation](/documentation/platform/pam/accounts/overview) on the account so the revealed password is replaced on a schedule, and rotate on demand after a checkout you want to close out immediately.

## Auditing

Every reveal writes a **View PAM Account Credentials** entry to the [audit log](/documentation/platform/pam/auditing) with the member, the account, the reason they gave, and when their approval expires. The log is the record of who holds a credential outside Infisical, so review it alongside session recordings.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Which account types can have their credentials viewed?">
    Any account that stores a credential. AWS IAM accounts are the exception: Infisical assumes a role rather than storing a secret, so there is nothing to reveal and the option does not appear.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a machine identity view a credential?">
    Yes, if it holds Operator or Admin on the folder or account, and an approval when the account is gated. An account whose template requires MFA stays out of reach, since no machine can complete the challenge.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does revoking a credential grant take the credential back?">
    No. Revoking stops further reveals, but a credential already copied is out of the platform and stays valid until it is rotated. Rotate the account to close that off.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does revoking a credential grant end the member's sessions?">
    No. Sessions are authorized by their own grant, so revoking credential access leaves live sessions running. Revoke the session grant to end those.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can an approver approve their own credential request?">
    No, the same separation of duties applies as for session requests. This is why the Operator role carries no approval rights: an Operator can request credential access but never clear their own request.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
