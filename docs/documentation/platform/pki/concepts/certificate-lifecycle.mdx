---
title: "Certificate lifecycle"
description: "Understand the stages a certificate goes through from discovery to retirement."
---

A certificate moves through several stages during its lifetime — from discovery to retirement. Not every stage is required. You can start issuing certificates without discovery, and many certificates simply expire without needing explicit revocation.

## Discovery

Find existing certificates across your infrastructure — web servers, load balancers, services, and devices. A complete inventory prevents outages from forgotten certificates and creates the foundation for automation and monitoring.

[Learn more about Certificate Discovery →](/documentation/platform/pki/discovery/overview)

## Enrollment

Request and issue a certificate from a CA. Enrollment can be **CSR-based** (client generates the key pair locally and submits a signing request) or **CSR-less** (CA generates the key pair and returns it with the certificate). Depending on your environment, enrollment can be manual via API or UI, or fully automated using protocols like ACME, EST, or SCEP.

[Learn more about Enrollment Methods →](/documentation/platform/pki/applications/enrollment-methods/overview)

## Approval

An optional human review step before certificates are issued. Approval policies are configured within an [Application](/documentation/platform/pki/applications/overview) for a specific certificate profile. Requests are placed in a pending state until the required approvers review and approve them. This enforces separation of duties, adds oversight for sensitive systems, and helps meet compliance requirements. [Machine identities](/documentation/platform/identities/machine-identities) can bypass approval when automated issuance is required.

[Learn more about Approval Policies →](/documentation/platform/pki/applications/approvals)

## Deployment

Install issued certificates on the systems that need them — web servers, load balancers, internal services. Deployment can be manual or automated through agents and pipelines. Certificate Syncs can automatically push certificates to cloud stores like AWS Secrets Manager, Azure Key Vault, and Cloudflare.

[Learn more about Certificate Syncs →](/documentation/platform/pki/applications/certificate-syncs/overview)

## Renewal

Replace a certificate before it expires to maintain trust and availability. Renewal can reuse the same key pair or rotate to a new one. In Infisical, renewal can be **server-driven** (Infisical auto-renews and syncs to external systems) or **client-driven** (an agent or workload initiates renewal when key material needs to stay under client control).

[Learn more about Certificate Renewal →](/documentation/platform/pki/applications/certificate-renewal)

## Revocation

Invalidate a certificate when it's compromised, misconfigured, or no longer needed. The CA publishes revocation status through CRLs (Certificate Revocation Lists) or OCSP (Online Certificate Status Protocol) so clients know not to trust the certificate. A new certificate can be issued and deployed as a replacement.

[Learn more about CRLs →](/documentation/platform/pki/ca/crl-distribution)

## Retirement

Deleting a certificate removes the certificate and its private key, and its details stay in the inventory as **Deleted**. Deleting can't be undone. A deleted certificate can't be renewed, so to replace it you issue a new one.

To bring a deleted certificate back under monitoring, [import it again](/documentation/platform/pki/applications/certificates#importing-a-deleted-certificate-again). The import adds a new certificate, and the deleted one stays in the inventory. A certificate with a revocation recorded in your organization can't be imported.

- **Revocation:** deleting doesn't revoke a certificate, but you can revoke it afterward if its certificate authority supports revocation
- **Re-imported certificates:** if you import a deleted certificate again into a profile with the same certificate authority, revoking either one revokes both, and the new certificate's permissions and approval policy apply
- **Copies without a certificate authority:** revoking a certificate also marks as revoked any copy of it in your organization that has no certificate authority, such as one you imported without a profile
- **Imported certificates:** revoke a certificate imported into a profile with an internal certificate authority before you delete it, because once it's deleted, the only way to revoke it is to import it again into a profile with the same certificate authority
- **Quota:** a deleted certificate issued from a certificate authority counts toward your certificate quota until it expires or is revoked (deleted imported and discovered certificates stop counting right away)
- **Cleanup:** Certificate Cleanup deletes expired certificates the same way, so their details also stay in the inventory as **Deleted**

[Learn more about Certificate Cleanup →](/documentation/platform/pki/settings/certificate-cleanup)
